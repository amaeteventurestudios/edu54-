Who is responsible for what

Data protection law gives different duties to the organisation that decides how personal data is used (the controller) and an organisation that handles it on the controller's behalf (a processor).

WhoRoleWhat that means
Your institutionControllerDecides who has an account, what is used and kept, and answers requests from students, parents and staff. Gives any notices and gets any consents the law requires.
EDU54Processor, only when we set up or support your boxActs only on your written instructions, keeps what we see confidential, and does not open student conversations, work or safety alerts.
EDU54Controller, for our own business dataWebsite, enquiries, orders and invoices, as set out in our Privacy Policy.

Because the box runs on your network and does not send student data to us, there are no sub-processors for student data.

In the admin area you can export one person's data (for an access or portability request), export your people list, delete a person, set how long tutor conversations are kept, back up the box, and read the audit log. Everyone signs in with their own account, and each role (student, teacher, safety contact, admin) sees only what it needs. Checks are enforced on the box itself, not just hidden in the screens.

We do not use your data for advertising and we do not train AI models on it. The AI models run on the box.

The laws we design for

EDU54 is sold across Africa, starting with English-speaking countries. These are the main laws schools raise with us. This is a plain summary, not legal advice; please check with your own adviser.

CountryLaw and regulatorPoints that matter for schools
NigeriaNigeria Data Protection Act 2023 and the General Application and Implementation Directive (GAID) 2025. Nigeria Data Protection Commission (NDPC).A child is anyone under 18: processing a child's data needs consent from a parent or guardian, unless another lawful basis applies. Controllers of major importance must register with the NDPC. Breaches likely to cause risk must be reported to the NDPC within 72 hours.
GhanaData Protection Act 2012 (Act 843). Data Protection Commission.Data controllers, including schools, must register with the Data Protection Commission. Processing a child's data generally needs a parent or guardian's consent. Data must be kept secure and no longer than needed.
KenyaData Protection Act 2019. Office of the Data Protection Commissioner.Education institutions processing personal data must register. Children's data needs parental consent and must be processed in the child's best interests.
South AfricaProtection of Personal Information Act (POPIA). Information Regulator.Children's information needs a competent person's consent or another listed ground. Responsible parties must appoint an information officer.
EU and UKGeneral Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and UK GDPR.Applies to institutions in Europe or offering services to people there. Requires a written processor agreement (Article 28), privacy notices and breach reporting within 72 hours. The UK Children's Code also applies to online services likely to be used by children.
United StatesFERPA and COPPA.For US schools, we act under the school's direct control as a "school official", and the school can authorise collection for educational use under COPPA.

What your institution should do

  1. Register with your data protection authority if the law requires it (for example the NDPC in Nigeria or the Data Protection Commission in Ghana).
  2. Tell people: add EDU54 to the privacy notice you give students, parents and staff. We can give you wording.
  3. Get consent where needed: for students under 18 in Nigeria, and for children elsewhere, collect any parent or guardian consent your law and policies require.
  4. Name safety contacts who will act on safety alerts.
  5. Set retention: confirm how long tutor conversations are kept, in the admin settings.
  6. Keep the box secure: a locked, ventilated place; strong admin passwords; regular backups kept safely.
  7. Remove leavers and handle requests using the export and delete tools.

Agreements we will sign

On request we provide, for your records or your data protection officer:

  • a data processing agreement covering our setup and support access, in line with the Nigeria Data Protection Act, the GDPR and similar laws;
  • a one-page data inventory and data flow for the box;
  • wording for your privacy notice to students, parents and staff;
  • written confirmation of data return and deletion if a box is returned to us for repair or at the end of its use.

Ask at privacy@edu54.com.

If something goes wrong

If we become aware of a security incident affecting personal data on your box or in our systems, we will tell your institution without undue delay, with what we know, what we are doing about it, and what you may need to do. That lets you meet your own deadlines (72 hours to the regulator in Nigeria, under the GDPR and in several other countries).

If you suspect a problem, contact privacy@edu54.com straight away.
